## Which smaller companies are winning share in AI data-center bottlenecks right now (2026)?

### Takeaway
The clearest 2026 share-winners are in high-speed connectivity: copper and optical interconnect (Credo, Semtech, AAOI), PCIe/scale-up switching (Astera), and precision timing (SiTime). There are also secondary plays in data-center power distribution (Powell) and AI memory/infrastructure integration (Penguin). Every one of them posted record revenue in its latest quarter, and several are limited by supply, not by demand.

### Inferences
- The bottleneck is moving from GPUs to connectivity: 800G going to 1.6T optics, AECs, PCIe 6 and scale-up switching. Three companies (CRDO, SMTC, AAOI) all cite the 1.6T ramp, so that transition is the key 2026-27 driver across the group.
- Power is a real bottleneck, but Powell's revenue lags its orders. Upside there depends on how fast it can execute and convert backlog, not on demand.
- PENG is the most "under the radar" name with a direct AI-memory link. Its results depend partly on memory pricing, which is a cyclical tailwind, not a structural one.

## What are the biggest risks (customer concentration, dilution, cyclicality, AI capex slowdown)?

The biggest risks are: extreme valuations and prior run-ups (ALAB, SITM, AAOI, CRDO); dilution (AAOI's $600M ATM); execution and capacity (AAOI, POWL); cyclicality of memory pricing (PENG); and a sector-wide AI-capex sentiment shock, which already produced a sharp September 2026 pullback. Customer concentration (hyperscalers) is structurally high across this group, but I could not confirm current percentages.

### "Already ran up / possibly priced in" flags
- **AAOI:** +390% in one year, dilutive ATM, thin ~30% gross margin. The highest risk and highest beta of the group.
- **ALAB:** ~$60B market cap at ~30-36x sales. Leadership is well-known.
- **SITM:** +127% growth, but ~31x run-rate sales, and the stock is near consensus targets on some trackers.
- **CRDO:** fundamentals are excellent but it is a $36-40B large-cap. It is not under the radar, though the pullback is partly de-rating it.
- **Less priced-in (relatively):** SMTC (lower multiple, analyst upgrades), PENG (cheap, but cyclical and lower margin), POWL (moderate P/E; upside depends on execution).

### Inferences
- A sensible way for a $1,000/month retail investor to size these names: split between higher-quality compounders bought on pullbacks (CRDO, SMTC) and one or two smaller, cheaper names (PENG, POWL), with AAOI held small if at all given dilution and volatility. This is a synthesis of the data above, not an analyst recommendation.
- All of these names are highly correlated with hyperscaler capex. Owning several does not diversify away the AI-capex-slowdown risk.
- The AAOI data contains an internal inconsistency to double-check:
  - 800G revenue of $12.8M is described as only 11.9% of data-center revenue (implying ~$108M of data-center revenue out of $192M total).
  - A ~$1.1B 2026 target, set against Q2 at $192M and Q3 at ~$272M (guide midpoint), implies a very large Q4 (roughly $450M+).
  - That is aggressive, and the report-writer should present it as management's target, not a forecast.
