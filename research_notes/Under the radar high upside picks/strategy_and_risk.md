# Strategy and Risk Framework: $1,000/month into a High-Risk Stock + Crypto Portfolio (US, 2026)

Educational framing only, not personalized advice. Research date: 2026-09-29. Note: sec.gov and investor.gov were blocked by the research proxy, so SEC material is cited from search-result summaries rather than full-text reads.

## DCA vs lump sum, and concentrated vs diversified speculative portfolios

### Takeaway
For a windfall, lump-sum investing beats a 12-month DCA schedule about two-thirds of the time, but for someone investing $1,000 out of each month's income, "DCA" is simply investing as the cash arrives, and it's the right default. Skewness research shows concentrated portfolios of a few speculative picks usually trail the index, because most individual stocks lose and a small minority drive all the gains.

### Cited Findings
- Vanguard: lump sum beat a 12-month DCA schedule in about 68% of historical periods across the US, UK and Australia, with a range of about 62–74% by region. The average advantage was about 1.2–2.4 percentage points, depending on the stock/bond mix. — [Vanguard, "Dollar-cost averaging just means taking risk later" (PDF)](https://static.twentyoverten.com/5980d16bbfb1c93238ad9c24/rJpQmY8o7/Dollar-Cost-Averaging-Just-Means-Taking-Risk-Later-Vanguard.pdf); [Vanguard NL, "The truth about cost averaging"](https://www.nl.vanguard/professional/vanguard-365/cost-averaging)
- Vanguard's framing: lump sum wins because markets rise more often than they fall, so cash left waiting gives up expected return. DCA trades some of that return for a smoother ride and less risk of regret from bad timing. — [Vanguard PDF](https://static.twentyoverten.com/5980d16bbfb1c93238ad9c24/rJpQmY8o7/Dollar-Cost-Averaging-Just-Means-Taking-Risk-Later-Vanguard.pdf)
- J.P. Morgan "Agony & Ecstasy" (1980–2020): more than 40% of stocks that ever joined the Russell 3000 suffered a "catastrophic loss". That means a drop of 70% or more from peak with little recovery, leaving a final loss from peak of at least 60%. About 66% of stocks underperformed the Russell 3000, and 42% had negative absolute returns. Only about 10% became "megawinners" with 500%+ cumulative excess return over the index. — [J.P. Morgan Eye on the Market (PDF)](https://privatebank.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpm-wm-aem/global/pb/en/insights/eye-on-the-market/eotm-the-agony-and-the-ecstasy.pdf)
- Broad cap-weighted indexes do well because they let the rare megawinners ride. — [J.P. Morgan PDF](https://privatebank.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpm-wm-aem/global/pb/en/insights/eye-on-the-market/eotm-the-agony-and-the-ecstasy.pdf)

### Inferences
- A $1,000/month plan is DCA by necessity, and the lump-sum-vs-DCA debate only matters if a lump sum (for example, a bonus) arrives. In that case the evidence favors investing it right away, unless the person would panic-sell after a quick drop.
- Positive skewness means a portfolio of 3–5 speculative stocks has a high chance of trailing the index even if it has a small chance of a big win. A diversified satellite (a small-cap growth ETF, or 15–25 names) keeps the lottery-ticket upside while lowering the odds of missing the rare winners.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous study of DCA specifically in crypto that is comparable to Vanguard's equity work.
- I didn't find a peer-reviewed study that quantifies the ideal number of speculative positions under extreme skew.

## What share of individual stocks and altcoins generate most returns (skewness base rates)

### Takeaway
Since 1926, only about 4% of US stocks account for all net stock-market wealth creation above T-bills, and most individual stocks lose to T-bills over their lifetimes. Crypto is even more extreme: more than half of all tokens ever launched are already "dead", and the failure count exploded in 2024–2025 with memecoin launchpads.

### Cited Findings
- Bessembinder (Journal of Financial Economics, 2018): four of every seven common stocks in CRSP since 1926 had lifetime buy-and-hold returns below one-month T-bills. The top roughly 4.3% of stocks (1,092 companies) account for all net wealth creation of the US stock market over T-bills. — [SSRN: Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2900447); [ASU W.P. Carey summary](https://wpcarey.asu.edu/department-finance/faculty-research/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills)
- Bessembinder's explanation: the market beats T-bills because of positive skewness (a few huge winners), not because the typical stock does well. — [Morningstar interview with Bessembinder](https://www.morningstar.com/personal-finance/hendrik-bessembinder-do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills)
- Russell 3000, 1980–2020: more than 40% of stocks had a catastrophic loss of 70% or more, and about 10% were megawinners. — [J.P. Morgan PDF](https://privatebank.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpm-wm-aem/global/pb/en/insights/eye-on-the-market/eotm-the-agony-and-the-ecstasy.pdf)
- CoinGecko: as of December 31, 2025, 53.2% of cryptocurrencies tracked on GeckoTerminal had failed. That's about 11.6 million "dead" tokens out of roughly 20.2 million projects. — [CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/how-many-cryptocurrencies-failed); [Crowdfund Insider summary](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/257221-dead-coins-over-50-of-cryptocurrencies-have-failed-report-reveals/)
- CoinGecko: 86.3% of failures from 2021–2025 happened in 2025 alone, including 7.7 million in Q4 2025. The count was only 2,584 in 2021, 213,075 in 2022 and 245,049 in 2023. The surge started in 2024 (about 1.4 million dead coins), driven by easy launchpads such as pump.fun. — [Crowdfund Insider / CoinGecko](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/257221-dead-coins-over-50-of-cryptocurrencies-have-failed-report-reveals/)

### Inferences
- The practical base rate is that a randomly chosen small-cap or speculative stock is more likely to lose badly (about 40% chance of a 70%+ permanent drawdown) than to become a 5x-plus winner (about 10%).
- The crypto failure figures are dominated by micro-cap memecoins. Retail buyers of top-100 coins face lower (but still high) failure rates. The report should separate "established large caps" (BTC, ETH) from the "long tail".

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous source for how often small caps or altcoins specifically reach 10x. The J.P. Morgan 500%-excess-return threshold (about 10%) is the closest proxy.
- I found no reliable survival rate for top-100-by-market-cap altcoins across cycles. That would be a useful follow-up.

## Allocation frameworks (core / satellite / moonshot), position sizing, rebalancing and profit-taking

### Takeaway
Core-satellite is the standard framework. Aggressive versions run about 60–70% core and 30–40% satellite, while Bogleheads-style guidance caps pure speculation at 10% or less. A common "60/30/10" mapping for this plan is 60% broad index core, 30% higher-growth diversified satellites, and 10% moonshots (individual small caps and altcoins).

### Cited Findings
- Core-satellite means holding most of the portfolio in passive index funds (the core) and putting smaller pieces into active or targeted strategies (satellites). — [Vanguard Australia core-satellite guide (PDF)](https://www.vanguard.com.au/content/dam/intl/australia/shared/documents/resources/Core_Satellite_brochure_2022.pdf); [Saxo guide](https://www.home.saxo/learn/guides/diversification/core-satellite-approach-a-smarter-way-to-diversify-your-investments)
- Aggressive investors might use about a 60/40 core/satellite split, which increases targeted exposure along with volatility and loss risk. — [CMC Markets](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en-gb/investing-guides/core-satellite-approach); [Saxo](https://www.home.saxo/learn/guides/diversification/core-satellite-approach-a-smarter-way-to-diversify-your-investments)
- Bogleheads forum consensus on aggressive young investors: keep the speculative portion to 10% or less. — [Bogleheads: Portfolio Review, Aggressive & Young](https://www.bogleheads.org/forum/viewtopic.php?t=465477); [Bogleheads: core and satellite](https://www.bogleheads.org/forum/viewtopic.php?t=338782)

### Inferences (illustrative monthly split of $1,000, not a recommendation)
- Core, about $600: a total US or S&P 500 index fund plus optional international.
- Growth satellite, about $300: for example, a small-cap growth ETF (VBK, 0.05% expense ratio), thematic ETFs, and a spot bitcoin/ether ETF or coin.
- Moonshot, about $100: individual speculative stocks and altcoins. Keep each position at 1–2% of total portfolio at cost, so any single failure is survivable (given the roughly 40% catastrophic-loss and 53% dead-coin base rates above).
- Rebalancing, which is standard practice rather than a single cited study: check once or twice a year, or use bands (for example, when a sleeve drifts more than 5 percentage points from target). Direct new monthly contributions to underweight sleeves first, which avoids selling and triggering taxes.
- Profit-taking rule of thumb: when a moonshot grows past about 2–3x its target weight, trim back to target and move the gains to the core. Because of skewness, don't sell winners too early. Trimming only the excess keeps exposure to a potential megawinner.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative academic source that endorses "60/30/10" specifically. It's a popular-press heuristic, and the report should present it that way.
- I didn't find a primary source with optimal rebalancing thresholds for crypto-heavy portfolios.

## Higher-upside but diversified vehicles, and leveraged ETF decay

### Takeaway
Small-cap growth ETFs, thematic ETFs and spot crypto ETFs offer diversified exposure to high-beta areas at low cost. Leveraged ETFs reset daily and suffer volatility decay, which regulators warn makes them unsuitable for buy-and-hold.

### Cited Findings
- VBK (Vanguard Small-Cap Growth) has a 0.05% expense ratio and a 5-year maximum drawdown of about -38.4%. IWO (iShares Russell 2000 Growth) has a 0.24% expense ratio and a 5-year maximum drawdown of about -40.5%. — [Motley Fool, VBK vs IWO (Aug 2026)](https://www.fool.com/coverage/etfs/2026/08/11/which-small-cap-growth-etf-is-better-vanguard-s-vbk-or-the-ishares-iwo/); [Vanguard VBK page](https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/vbk/vanguard-small-cap-growth-etf)
- IBIT (iShares Bitcoin Trust) and FBTC (Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin) each charge a 0.25% net expense ratio and hold spot bitcoin. FBTC keeps custody in-house at Fidelity Digital Assets, while IBIT uses Coinbase as third-party custodian. IBIT is the largest spot bitcoin ETF, with about $70.6 billion in AUM. — [Motley Fool, FBTC vs IBIT (Apr 2026)](https://www.fool.com/coverage/etfs/2026/04/13/what-s-the-difference-between-the-fbtc-and-ibit-bitcoin-etfs/); [NerdWallet spot bitcoin ETF list](https://www.nerdwallet.com/investing/learn/spot-bitcoin-etf)
- SEC and FINRA joint alert: most leveraged and inverse ETFs reset daily, so over periods longer than a day their returns can differ significantly from the stated multiple, especially in volatile markets. They are "specialized products with extra risks for buy-and-hold investors." — [SEC Investor Bulletin: Leveraged and Inverse ETFs](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/leveragedetfs-alert); [InvestmentNews on the SEC/FINRA alert](https://www.investmentnews.com/etfs/sec-joins-finra-in-warning-about-use-of-leveraged-and-inverse-etfs/23375)
- The SEC's Investor Advisory Committee (2023) issued recommendations on single-stock and leveraged ETFs, reflecting ongoing regulatory concern. — [SEC IAC recommendation (PDF)](https://www.sec.gov/files/spotlight/iac/20230622-recommendation-single-stock-etfs-and-leveraged-etfs.pdf)

### Inferences
- Decay math (standard arithmetic, illustrative): an index goes +10% then -10% and ends at -1%. A 2x daily-reset fund goes +20% then -20% and ends at -4%, which is 4x the loss rather than 2x. Choppy, volatile markets erode leveraged funds even when the index ends flat.
- If leveraged ETFs are used at all, they belong only in the moonshot sleeve, at small size, with the understanding that they can lose most of their value in a sideways market.

### Gaps
- The SEC DERA economics note on leveraged ETF return distributions (https://www.sec.gov/files/dera_letf_economics_note_nov2019.pdf) was blocked by the proxy, so its quantitative findings aren't included.
- I didn't capture ARKK or other thematic-ETF drawdown figures.

## Account choice (Roth IRA vs taxable, crypto in IRAs), and holding money "given to Dom"

### Takeaway
For 2026, the IRA limit is $7,500 ($8,600 at age 50+), which absorbs about $625 of the $1,000 per month. A Roth is the natural home for the highest-upside assets because gains grow tax-free, while the remainder goes to a taxable brokerage account. Crypto can go in a Roth through spot ETFs or dedicated crypto IRAs. Money given to Dom is a gift of up to $19,000 per year without paperwork. It can sit in Dom's own account, a joint account, or a UTMA account if Dom is a minor.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 IRA contribution limit: $7,500 (up from $7,000), plus a $1,100 catch-up for age 50+, for $8,600 total. — [IRS newsroom](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500); [IRS Notice 2025-67 (PDF)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf)
- 2026 Roth income phase-outs (MAGI): $153,000–$168,000 for single or head of household, $242,000–$252,000 for married filing jointly, and $0–$10,000 for married filing separately. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/13/roth-ira-income-limits-2026.html); [Vanguard](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/iras/roth-ira-income-limits)
- Fidelity Crypto IRA (Roth, traditional or rollover) lets investors buy bitcoin, ether and litecoin directly, custodied by Fidelity Digital Assets in cold storage, with no account fee. — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/04/02/fidelity-lets-investors-directly-invest-in-crypto-through-new-retirement-plan); [Fidelity: Crypto IRAs explained](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/crypto/ccl-crypto-and-iras-2026-vid)
- Alto CryptoIRA (self-directed, Roth or traditional) offers 250+ assets through Coinbase, with no monthly fee but a 1% fee per crypto trade. — [LendEDU Alto review](https://lendedu.com/blog/alto-crypto-ira-review/); [Alto](https://www.altoira.com/news/alto-coinbase-safety)
- Gifting: the 2026 annual gift tax exclusion is $19,000 per recipient ($38,000 for a married couple). Gifts above that require Form 709 but reduce the $15 million lifetime exemption rather than triggering tax. — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/gift-tax-exclusion); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/10/irs-announces-increased-gift-and-estate-tax-exemption-amounts-for-2026)
- UTMA/UGMA custodial accounts (if Dom is a minor): in 2026 the first $1,350 of a child's unearned income is tax-free, the next $1,350 is taxed at the child's rate, and anything above $2,700 is taxed at the parent's rate (the kiddie tax). A parent-owned or joint brokerage account gets no kiddie-tax treatment, so gains are taxed at the owner's rates. UTMA assets irrevocably belong to the child and transfer at the state age of majority. — [Fidelity: Kiddie tax](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/kiddie-tax); [Fidelity: UGMA/UTMA](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/custodial-account-for-kids); [Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/saving-college-custodial-accounts)

### Inferences
- Account order (illustrative): (1) Roth IRA at about $625/month, holding the highest-expected-growth assets (small-cap growth, spot crypto ETFs, speculative stocks), since tax-free compounding is worth most on the biggest winners. (2) Taxable brokerage for the remaining $375/month, holding the broad index core, which is tax-efficient and qualifies for long-term capital gains rates.
- Caveat for the Roth: losses inside a Roth can't be harvested, and a zeroed Roth position permanently uses up contribution room. Some investors therefore prefer holding the most binary bets in taxable accounts, where losses offset gains and up to $3,000 of ordinary income.
- "Giving $1,000 to Dom to save": at $12,000/year, the gift stays under the $19,000 exclusion. Options:
  - (a) If Dom is an adult with earned income: Dom opens his own Roth IRA and taxable account, and the giver sets up a recurring monthly ACH transfer. Roth contributions require Dom to have earned income at least equal to the contribution.
  - (b) A joint brokerage account (JTWROS) gives both parties visibility and control, but either owner can withdraw the money.
  - (c) If Dom is a minor: a UTMA account (control passes at majority), or a custodial Roth IRA if Dom has earned income.
  - Automation: most brokerages support recurring deposits plus automatic investment into chosen funds on a set day each month, which removes timing decisions.

### Gaps
- Dom's age, income and relationship to the giver are unknown, and these determine the best structure. The report should present it conditionally.
- The Roth contribution earned-income requirement is well-established IRS rule (IRS Pub 590-A), but I didn't fetch it this session.

## Current 2026 tax rules: short vs long-term gains, crypto treatment, wash sales, reporting

### Takeaway
Gains on assets held more than one year are taxed at 0%, 15% or 20%. Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income. The IRS treats crypto as property, and as of September 2026 the wash-sale rule still doesn't apply to crypto (a bill was introduced in June 2026 but hasn't passed). Starting with 2026 transactions, crypto brokers must report cost basis on Form 1099-DA for covered assets.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 long-term capital gains brackets (Rev. Proc. 2025-32), by taxable income:
  - 0% rate: up to $49,450 single, $98,900 married filing jointly, $66,200 head of household.
  - 15% rate: up to $545,500 single, $613,700 married filing jointly, $579,600 head of household.
  - 20% rate: above those amounts.
  — [Tax Foundation 2026 brackets](https://taxfoundation.org/data/all/federal/2026-tax-brackets/); [The College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/23577/capital-gains-tax-brackets/)
- Wash-sale rule (IRC §1091) applies to stocks and securities but not to crypto, because the IRS treats crypto as property. As of 2026 no enacted statute extends it. H.R. 9172, the "Applying Existing Tax Anti-Abuse Rules to Digital Assets Act," was introduced on June 8, 2026, referred to Ways and Means, and is not yet law. — [ChainWise CPA](https://chainwisecpa.com/crypto-wash-sale-2026/); [Gordon Law](https://gordonlaw.com/learn/crypto-wash-sale-rule-in-2026-is-the-loophole-finally-closed/); [TurboTax](https://ttlc.intuit.com/turbotax-support/en-us/help-article/cryptocurrency/wash-sale-rule-cryptocurrency/L1d6BuQpH_US_en_US)
- The wash-sale rule does apply to spot bitcoin ETFs (IBIT, FBTC), since they are securities.
  - Source status: this is an inference from the fact that the rule covers securities (see sources above). I didn't find an explicit source.
- Form 1099-DA reporting:
  - For 2025 transactions (forms issued in early 2026), brokers report gross proceeds only.
  - For transactions on or after January 1, 2026, brokers must also report cost basis for "covered" digital assets, meaning assets acquired from 2026 onward and held in the same custodial broker account.
  - FIFO is the default lot method, though specific identification is allowed.
  - Only a small share of transactions (perhaps about 5%) may be covered in the first year.
  — [The Tax Adviser (Mar 2026)](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2026/mar/navigating-the-form-1099-da-reporting-maze/); [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/form-1099-da-debut-will-test-broker-taxpayer-readiness-in-transition-year/)

### Inferences
- Holding speculative winners for more than 12 months before selling can cut the federal rate from ordinary income (up to 37%) to 15% for most middle-income filers. That matters a lot for profit-taking rules.
- Crypto tax-loss harvesting (sell at a loss, then rebuy immediately) is currently legal for coins held directly but not for crypto ETFs. The report should flag the risk that H.R. 9172 or similar legislation passes.
- Anyone moving coins between exchanges will still need tracking software, because transferred coins are generally not "covered".

### Gaps
- I didn't retrieve 2026 ordinary-income (short-term) bracket thresholds. The Tax Foundation link above has them.
- The $3,000 annual capital-loss deduction limit against ordinary income is long-standing law (IRS Topic 409), but I didn't fetch it this session.
- The 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax thresholds weren't verified this session.

## Brokerages / exchanges and fees

### Takeaway
Stock and ETF trades are commission-free at major brokers (Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard, Robinhood). For crypto, the cheapest options for small monthly buys are spot ETFs (0.25%/year) inside a brokerage or IRA, or "pro" exchange interfaces. Simple "buy" buttons and spread-based apps can quietly cost 0.5–2.5% per trade.

### Cited Findings
- Coinbase: simple purchases cost 0–0.60% depending on asset and volume. Coinbase Advanced charges 0.15% maker / 0.25% taker at base tier. — [CoinLedger Robinhood vs Coinbase](https://coinledger.io/tools/robinhood-vs-coinbase); [TokenTax lowest-fee exchanges](https://tokentax.co/blog/crypto-exchange-with-lowest-fees)
- Kraken: up to 1% on simple purchases and 0–0.4% on Kraken Pro. Kraken+ costs $4.99/month for fee-free trading up to $10,000. — [CoinLedger Kraken vs Robinhood](https://coinledger.io/tools/kraken-vs-robinhood-crypto); [Kraken](https://www.kraken.com/learn/lowest-fee-crypto-exchange)
- Robinhood Crypto: no explicit commission, but a spread markup embedded in the price, reported at about 0.5–2.5% depending on volatility and liquidity. — [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/tools/kraken-vs-robinhood-crypto)
  - Source caveat: the spread range comes from a third-party estimate and may vary.
- Spot bitcoin ETFs (IBIT, FBTC) cost 0.25% per year and trade commission-free at brokers. — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/etfs/2026/04/13/what-s-the-difference-between-the-fbtc-and-ibit-bitcoin-etfs/)
- Fidelity Crypto IRA has no account fee. Alto CryptoIRA charges 1% per trade. — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/04/02/fidelity-lets-investors-directly-invest-in-crypto-through-new-retirement-plan); [LendEDU](https://lendedu.com/blog/alto-crypto-ira-review/)

### Inferences
- On a $100 monthly altcoin buy, a 1–2.5% spread or fee costs $1–$2.50 each time, which is $12–$30 a year. That's small in dollars but large relative to an ETF's 0.25%/year. Limit orders on pro interfaces reduce the cost.
- A practical setup: one mainstream brokerage (Roth plus taxable, with automatic investing and spot crypto ETFs), plus at most one reputable exchange for altcoins that aren't available as ETFs.

### Gaps
- I didn't verify each major broker's current (2026) stock commission policy or its fractional-share and automatic-investing support in this session.
- I didn't research exchange security records or proof-of-reserves.

## Projection math: $1,000/month over 5, 10 and 20 years

### Takeaway
Contributions alone total $60,000, $120,000 and $240,000 over 5, 10 and 20 years. At 7–10% per year (roughly historical US stock returns), 20 years produces about $0.5–$0.72 million. The high-risk "dream" rates of 15–20% produce $1.3–$2.4 million, but the skewness base rates above show those outcomes are unlikely for most concentrated portfolios.

### Cited Findings (calculated; formula FV = P × ((1+m)^n − 1)/m, with monthly rate m = (1+r)^(1/12) − 1, $1,000 deposited at each month-end, before taxes and fees)

| Annual return | 5 years | 10 years | 20 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| -5% (bad outcome) | $53,037 | $94,076 | $150,402 |
| 0% | $60,000 | $120,000 | $240,000 |
| 4% | $66,179 | $146,696 | $363,842 |
| 7% | $71,196 | $171,052 | $507,536 |
| 10% | $76,561 | $199,864 | $718,259 |
| 15% | $86,331 | $259,973 | $1,311,707 |
| 20% | $97,216 | $339,119 | $2,438,856 |

- Calculation: done by the researcher in Python. These are deterministic, nominal and unadjusted for inflation. They aren't forecasts.

### Inferences
- Over 20 years, the gap between 7% and 10% (about $210,000) is bigger than total contributions over 10 years. Keeping fees low and staying invested matters more than picking the "right" moonshot.
- A real portfolio won't earn a smooth rate. Volatility and the order of returns shift the outcome, and a 60/30/10 portfolio's realized return is dominated by the 60% core.

### Gaps
- I didn't retrieve a sourced long-run historical return figure for the S&P 500 or small-cap growth. The report writer should label the 7–10% range as an assumption or cite another researcher's figure.
- The projections don't include inflation or taxes.
